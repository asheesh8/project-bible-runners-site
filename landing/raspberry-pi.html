Overview

What is the VillageServer Pi?

A Raspberry Pi running a local web server. Plug in microSD cards loaded with language libraries, power it on, and it broadcasts a Wi-Fi network. Anyone within range can join that network and open the library in a phone browser — like having a small offline internet made entirely of Scripture and gospel resources.

What the Pi provides

  • Auto-broadcasting Wi-Fi hotspot — turns on the moment the Pi powers up, no router or internet needed
  • An offline library server — reachable at 10.42.0.1 from any phone browser, no app to install
  • MicroSD-based language libraries — the language and resources available are determined by the cards plugged into USB readers
  • Four content sections per language — Bible, Audio, Video, and Books, browsable and downloadable straight from the phone
  • Fully offline operation — works indefinitely with no internet connection, anywhere there's power

For the field

Using the library — step by step

This is all a person in the field needs to know.

1

Power on the VillageServer

Plug in the power bank or power supply. Wait about 60 seconds for the Pi to boot. The Wi-Fi network appears automatically.

2

Join the Wi-Fi network

Open Settings → Wi-Fi and select VillageServer. Password: village123.

Note: your phone may warn "no internet" — that's expected. Tap "Stay connected."
3

Open the library

Open any browser and type 10.42.0.1 in the address bar. The library home loads instantly.

4

Choose a language and browse

Tap a language card, then tap Bible, Audio, Video, or Books. Read, listen, or watch without saving anything first.

5

Download to share later

Hold any file link to save it. Share to other phones via AirDrop, Quick Share, LocalSend, Bluetooth, or USB — no Wi-Fi needed.

Troubleshooting

Common issues

Phone joins Wi-Fi but the page won't load

Double-check the address bar shows exactly 10.42.0.1 — not a search. Some phones auto-correct it into Google instead of visiting the address. Retype it manually.

Phone warns "no internet connection"

That's expected — the VillageServer network is intentionally offline. Tap "Stay connected" or "Use anyway" and continue.

Wi-Fi network isn't showing up

Give the Pi a full 60 seconds after power-on. If it still doesn't appear, power-cycle it (unplug, wait 10 seconds, plug back in).

A language is missing

The microSD card for that language may not be seated. Check that the USB reader is fully plugged in and power-cycle the Pi.
