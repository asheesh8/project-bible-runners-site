For schools and children's programs in Kenya

The whole Bible library, on one card

Scripture, audio Bible, gospel films, and teaching material for children — loaded onto a single microSD card that works on an ordinary phone, with no internet and no data. If you lead a school or a children's programme in Kenya serving children aged 6 to 12, you can apply for one.

Most schools we speak to already have what the card needs: a teacher or a head with a phone. That is the whole requirement. The card slots in, the library opens, and it keeps working when the power does not.

Apply for a card →

About 15 minutes. You will need a referee and your ministry or school credentials to hand.

What is on the card

  • Scripture in the languages your pupils actually read — Swahili and English, and other Kenyan languages where the translation exists.
  • Audio Bible — for the children who are still learning to read, and for the ones who never will.
  • Gospel films and children's teaching — material made for the 6 to 12 age group, not adult teaching handed down.
  • Everything offline — no internet, no data bundle, no monthly cost. Once the card is in your hand, nothing else is owed.

What we send, and what you already have

We want to be plain about this before you spend time on an application, because a promise we cannot keep helps nobody.

What we send you

  • A microSD card carrying the full offline library, loaded for your pupils' languages
  • An SD card adapter, so the card fits a phone, a reader, or a computer

What the school provides

  • A phone, tablet, or computer to read the card — a single teacher's phone is enough to begin
  • A television or projector, if you want to show films to a whole class or assembly
  • Speakers, power, and anything else a larger showing needs

We are not able to send equipment or funding at this stage. The card is genuinely where this starts — schools that begin with one teacher's phone and later add a screen are the ordinary path, not the exception.

Who this is for

This is a good fit if you lead or coordinate a school, a children's church programme, an orphanage, or a community learning centre in Kenya, and children between 6 and 12 are in your care regularly — not once, but week after week.

We will ask who you are. The application is made by a named person who is answerable for the children: a head teacher, a programme coordinator, a pastor, or a school director. Not a general enquiry address.

What happens after you apply

Laura handles every application personally from here. She is our receptionist, and she will be the person you hear from.

  1. She reads your application

    Usually within a few days. If something is missing or does not quite add up, she writes and asks — she will not let a file sit in silence.

  2. She verifies your ministry

    A referee, your identification, your pastoral or school credentials, and photographs of your work. If sending documents would put you or your community at risk, say so on the form and she will verify you through two referees and your conversation instead.

  3. You speak with her

    A short conversation about your school and your pupils — twenty minutes or so, by WhatsApp, phone, or video, at a time that suits you. Everyone we send to speaks with Laura first. There is nothing to prepare.

  4. Your card is prepared and posted

    Loaded for your pupils' languages and sent to the address you gave her. She will not promise you a date she cannot keep, and she will tell you plainly where things stand.

Common questions

  • Does it cost anything? No. The card and the adapter are given, not sold. There is no subscription and nothing to renew.
  • Can the card be copied? Yes. Once it reaches you, the library can be copied onto other phones — that is how one card reaches a staff room rather than one teacher.
  • What if our school has no internet at all? That is the point of it. Nothing on the card needs a connection.
  • Do we need to be a registered school? No, but we do need to know who you are and who vouches for you. A children's programme run out of a church is as welcome as a registered academy.
